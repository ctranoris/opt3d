# A better sea

Date: 2026-11-03 18:15

## What changed

All of this is in the water shader and the wave tables in `viewer.js`.

1. **Waves.** The four sine waves are replaced by five Gerstner waves.
   - The crests are drawn together, so they are sharp and the troughs are
     broad.
   - The waves run toward the south, downwind of the default northerly,
     spread either side of it.
   - The wavelengths run from 4.6 m down to 1.15 m.
   - Each wave travels at its deep-water speed for its length, slowed a
     little to suit the scale of the scene.
   - The GLSL wave code is generated from the same `WAVES` table as
     `waveHeight()`, so the boat, the marks and the fleet float on exactly
     the waves that are drawn.
2. **The sea builds with the wind.** Wave heights scale from 0.6× in no
   wind to 1.2× at 18 kn. The change is eased over a few seconds, so the
   sea doesn't jump when the wind slider moves.
3. **Per-pixel normals.**
   - The shading normal is worked out per pixel from the big waves.
   - Seven moving ripples, from 0.9 m down to 0.1 m long, are added to it.
     Each fades out with distance before it gets too fine to show.
   - These replace the fixed sin × sin ripple pattern, which made a
     visible grid.
4. **Reflections show the clouds.** The reflection now samples the sky
   texture itself, clouds included, instead of the plain sky gradient.
5. **Water colour.**
   - The water is darker looking straight down into it and lighter at
     grazing angles.
   - Wave crests glow turquoise where light shines through them, most
     strongly when looking toward the sun.
6. **Whitecaps.** They start at about 10 kn and grow with the wind. They
   are sparse, broken up by noise, and only on the highest crests, and
   they use the same foam texture as the wake.
7. **Sun glitter** toned down slightly.
8. **README:** the description of the sea has been updated.
